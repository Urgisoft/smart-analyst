# 02 — Fundamentals

> Fundamentals tell you what the underlying business or holdings actually look like. For stocks: company earnings, growth, profitability, balance sheet. For ETFs and funds: aggregate fundamentals of the underlying holdings.

## For individual stocks

### Profitability metrics
- EPS (earnings per share) trailing 12 months — actual recent profitability
- EPS forward 12 months — analyst consensus for next year
- EPS growth rate (TTM vs prior TTM) — pace of earnings expansion
- Profit margin trend — expanding, stable, or contracting
- Return on equity (ROE) — how efficiently the company uses shareholder capital

### Growth metrics
- Revenue growth (TTM vs prior TTM)
- Revenue growth (5-year average)
- Earnings growth (5-year average)
- Forecast growth (analyst consensus, 3-5 year)

### Balance sheet
- Debt-to-equity ratio
- Cash and equivalents on hand
- Interest coverage ratio (EBIT / interest expense)
- Free cash flow (FCF) — actual cash the business generates

### Earnings track record
- Beat/miss pattern over recent quarters
- Magnitude of beats/misses — small or large surprises
- Guidance: does management raise or lower guidance over time

## For ETFs and funds

### Aggregate portfolio metrics (weighted average of holdings)
- Portfolio P/E (forward and trailing) — overall valuation level
- Portfolio P/B (price-to-book)
- Portfolio P/S (price-to-sales)
- Portfolio P/CF (price-to-cash-flow)
- Historical earnings growth of the holdings
- Forecast earnings growth of the holdings
- Dividend yield

### Compare to category and benchmark
Absolute numbers are useless without context. Always pull the same metrics for the category median and the index/benchmark for comparison. Morningstar's portfolio page makes this easy.

## Where to find this data

| Source | What it gives you | Notes |
|--------|-------------------|-------|
| Fidelity research page | Most metrics in one place | Best starting point if you have a Fidelity account |
| Morningstar | Portfolio fundamentals, style measures | Free for basics, paid for premium |
| Yahoo Finance | Stock fundamentals, earnings dates | Free, decent quality |
| Company 10-K (SEC EDGAR) | Annual report, complete financials | Authoritative source, dense |
| FactSet Earnings Insight | S&P 500 sector aggregates | Free weekly PDF |

## How to interpret

**Strong fundamentals look like:** growing earnings (15%+ for individual stocks, 10%+ for sectors), expanding margins, manageable debt, FCF that supports the valuation, beating analyst estimates more often than missing.

**Concerning fundamentals look like:** declining earnings, contracting margins, debt growing faster than earnings, FCF that doesn't support the valuation, repeatedly missing analyst estimates, lowered guidance.

> Note: "Strong fundamentals" doesn't mean "buy." Strong fundamentals at a high price can be a worse trade than weak fundamentals at a cheap price. Fundamentals tell you about quality. [[03-valuation]] tells you about price.
