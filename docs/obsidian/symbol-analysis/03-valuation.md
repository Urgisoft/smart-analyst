# 03 — Valuation

> Valuation answers: am I paying a reasonable price for what I'm getting? Same metrics work for stocks and ETFs, just applied at different levels. Always compare to peer group, historical average, and broad market.

## The valuation ratios that matter

| Ratio | What it measures | Lower vs higher |
|-------|------------------|-----------------|
| P/E (trailing) | Price ÷ last 12 months earnings | Lower = cheaper relative to recent earnings |
| P/E (forward) | Price ÷ next 12 months expected earnings | Lower = cheaper relative to expected earnings |
| P/B (price/book) | Price ÷ accounting book value | Lower = closer to liquidation value |
| P/S (price/sales) | Price ÷ revenue | Useful when earnings volatile or negative |
| P/FCF (price/free cash flow) | Price ÷ cash flow generated | Less manipulable than P/E |
| EV/EBITDA | Enterprise value ÷ operating earnings | Accounts for debt and cash |
| PEG ratio | P/E ÷ earnings growth rate | Growth-adjusted valuation |
| Dividend yield | Annual dividend ÷ price | Income return component |

## The three comparisons that matter

### 1. Versus peer group / sector median
How does this symbol compare to other similar symbols today? A trailing P/E of 34 means very different things for a technology ETF (peer median ~25) versus a utility ETF (peer median ~15).

**FTEC example:** P/E 34.51 vs sector median 24.49 = 13th percentile (more expensive than 87% of peers).

### 2. Versus its own historical average
How does this symbol compare to its own past valuations? A P/E of 25 might be cheap if the historical average is 30, but expensive if the historical average is 18.

**NVDA example:** current TTM P/E 45 vs 5-year average 65 → cheap on its own history despite high absolute number.

### 3. Versus the broad market
How does this symbol compare to the overall market? The S&P 500 typically trades at 18-20x forward earnings in normal periods. Anything significantly above is paying a growth premium; anything significantly below is either a value opportunity or a value trap.

## Growth-adjusted valuation (PEG)

Raw P/E ignores growth. A company growing 5% per year deserves a lower P/E than one growing 30%. PEG ratio (P/E ÷ growth rate) adjusts for this:

- **PEG below 1.0** = cheap given growth
- **PEG 1.0 - 2.0** = reasonable for high-growth companies
- **PEG above 2.0** = expensive even after accounting for growth

> FTEC PEG calculation: 34.51 / 26.26% historical growth = 1.31. Moderately elevated, not extreme.

## Where to find this data

- Fidelity research → Stock/ETF analyst tab
- Morningstar Portfolio page (for funds) or Valuation tab (for stocks)
- Yahoo Finance Statistics tab
- FactSet Earnings Insight for sector aggregates

## The honest valuation framework

> **🟢 Cheap on all three comparisons (peers, history, market) AND fundamentals are strong** = compelling buy candidate.
>
> **🟡 Cheap on history but expensive vs peers** = sector-wide rerating; consider why.
>
> **🔴 Expensive on all three AND fundamentals decelerating** = sell or avoid.
>
> **❓ Mixed signals** = need more context (technicals, sentiment, structural factors).
