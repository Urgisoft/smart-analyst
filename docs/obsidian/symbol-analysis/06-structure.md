# 06 — Structural Factors

> Structure tells you what you actually own and how it behaves under stress. For individual stocks: corporate structure, share class, business model. For ETFs/funds: holdings concentration, geographic exposure, factor tilts.

## Concentration analysis (critical for ETFs/funds)

### Holdings concentration
- Top 1 holding weight
- Top 3 holdings combined weight
- Top 10 holdings combined weight
- Number of holdings

**FTEC example:** NVDA 18.83% + AAPL 14.29% + MSFT 9.91% = 43% in top 3. Top 10 = ~60%. Out of 286 holdings, 60% of the fund is in 10 names. Effective concentration is closer to a 10-stock portfolio than to a 286-stock portfolio.

### Sector concentration
- Primary sector weight
- Top 3 sectors combined
- Number of sectors with >5% weight

### Geographic concentration
- Primary country weight
- Developed vs emerging markets split
- Currency exposure (matters for international holdings)

### Factor exposures

Morningstar's Factor Profile shows where the fund sits on:
- Style: Value ↔ Growth
- Size: Small ↔ Large
- Yield: Low ↔ High
- Momentum: Low ↔ High
- Quality: Low ↔ High
- Volatility: Low ↔ High
- Liquidity: Low ↔ High

## For individual stocks
- Business model — what does the company actually do?
- Revenue diversification — concentrated in one product/customer/geography?
- Competitive position — leader, challenger, niche player?
- Moat — what protects the business from competition (patents, network effects, brand, switching costs)?
- Management — tenure, track record, capital allocation history
- Insider ownership — meaningful insider stake ("skin in the game")?

## Liquidity & tradeability
- Average daily volume (shares)
- Average daily dollar volume (shares × price)
- Bid-ask spread (tight = liquid, wide = illiquid)
- Premium/discount to NAV (for ETFs) — should be near zero for liquid ETFs
- Tracking error (for index funds) — how closely does it follow the index?

## Costs and tax efficiency
- Expense ratio (for funds)
- Tax cost ratio — historical drag from distributions
- Annual turnover — how often holdings change (higher = more taxable distributions)
- Distribution schedule — when dividends/capital gains are paid
- K-1 vs 1099 reporting — K-1 forms complicate taxes

## How to interpret structure

> **🟢 Well-structured for ownership:** diversified holdings, low expenses, tax-efficient, high liquidity, factor profile matches your goal.

> **🟡 Concentration warning:** top 10 holdings >50% of fund, single sector >80% of fund, single country >90% of fund. Not necessarily bad — but you should KNOW you have concentrated exposure.

> **🔴 Structural problems:** very low liquidity (wide spreads, low volume), high expenses (>0.5% for index funds), high tax inefficiency, complex tax reporting, persistent premium/discount to NAV.
